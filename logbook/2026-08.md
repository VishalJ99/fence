# August 2026

## 2026-08-01 — PER-414 Tab notch-companion prototype

### Objective

Test whether a permission-isolated companion can occupy the smallest practical area beneath a notched Mac display, follow the pointer, and express escalating concern without changing Fence's binary or permissions.

### Method

- Started from verified Fence 3.4.11 commit `8ce8e4f` in a separate clean checkout; the dirty `/Users/vishaljain/selfcontrol` reference checkout was preserved.
- Added the independent Xcode app `Tab.app` in implementation commit `d43b0a20c8c73766a860a69359d4e1fafac43830`.
- Derived placement from `NSScreen.safeAreaInsets` and the auxiliary top areas, then drew a 44 x 20-point AppKit panel.
- Polled `NSEvent.mouseLocation` at 30 Hz with exponential smoothing.
- Added click-cycled neutral, mild-concern, and very-concerned chibi expressions. Mild concern raises straight high-contrast inner grey brows; very concern rotates the full straight brows inner-down and adds smaller pupils, a brief wiggle, and four separate coral curves at the top-right temple with a state-only right gutter. Each curve is displaced 10 percent farther from the motif center and the motif is rotated 20 degrees clockwise.
- Built and tested using the commands in `Tab/dist/reproduction.txt`, then launched the exact packaged app and exercised the click cycle with Computer Use.

### Observations

- The built-in MacBook Pro display reported a 2056 x 1329-point screen, 38-point top safe area, and a 220 x 38-point physical-notch exclusion centered between the auxiliary areas.
- The live Tab window reported a compact 44 x 20-point neutral/mild footprint at status-bar window layer 25, directly beneath the housing. Very concerned alone extends 6 points to the right for a 50 x 20-point footprint while leaving the eye positions fixed.
- The pointer-following eyes rendered and moved without a permission prompt.
- Computer Use reported the complete click sequence `neutral -> mild concern -> very concerned -> neutral`; the user approved the initial eyes and mild-concern direction and requested the inverted very-concern brows.
- At the real 44 x 20-point render size, the first tiny translucent stress mark clipped at the top-right boundary and read as a red blur. A thicker four-elbow revision became visible but formed the wrong flower/star silhouette and obscured the right eye and brow.
- The supplied chibi reference resolved the intended mark: four disconnected rounded curves bowing toward an empty center. The first reference-matched placement used an 8-point gutter and kept the mark fully separate from the face.
- Direct visual feedback favored a more attached temple cue. Moving the hatch 4 points upward and 3 points left gives it approximately 20 percent overlap at the moving eye/brow boundary without hiding either feature. That overlap permits a 6-point gutter; the live very-concerned capture measured 50 x 20 pixels.
- A later visual pass found that the four curves still merged perceptually. Moving each complete curve radially outward by 10 percent—without changing its size or 1.8-point stroke—increased the negative space. The requested 45-degree rotation was refined interactively to a subtler 20 degrees clockwise before validation.
- The user clarified that “remove the curvature in the eye balls” referred to the eyebrows. Replacing both brow Bezier paths with straight round-capped segments preserved the mild/very slope inversion while making the expression crisper; the round eye shapes were unchanged.
- All 14 tests passed on macOS 26.5.2 arm64. Xcode emitted only its deployment-version warning for the bundled XCTest framework while testing a macOS 12 target.
- The packaged app's identifier is `app.usefence.tab`; its sole entitlement is `com.apple.security.app-sandbox`; deep signature verification passed with a local ad-hoc signature.

### Interpretation

The small notch-companion interaction is mechanically viable without Screen Recording, Accessibility, microphone, Automation, network, daemon, or Fence integration. Grey brows keep concern character-led, while the reference-matched coral hatch supplies a high-heat cue without turning the whole face into an alarm. At this scale, a controlled edge overlap makes the mark feel attached to the character and uses less space than either full separation or drawing over the face. Click-to-cycle is useful for UI tuning but should be revisited when a real accountability state machine owns expressions.

### Next steps

- Keep the user-approved three-tier UI frozen while backend context sensing is developed on a separate ticket and branch.
- Replace temporary click control with explicit state input once the heat model exists.
- Evaluate structured foreground-app/window context before sending pixels; gate ScreenCaptureKit and network access separately and do not add those permissions to Fence.

## 2026-08-01 — PER-416 Tab live screen-context proof

### Objective

Verify that the separate Tab binary can observe changing macOS work at up to one local frame per second, expose freshness and process-resource measurements in a side log, and obtain a low-latency structured classification without sending every captured frame to a remote model.

### Method

- Kept the PER-414 notch UI unchanged and added an opt-in sidecar enabled by `--screen-context-log` or `TAB_SCREEN_CONTEXT_LOG=1`.
- Added ScreenCaptureKit capture at a 1024-pixel maximum long edge and one frame per second, excluding Tab's own process and cursor.
- Added a 32 x 18 local grayscale change signature and fast Apple Vision OCR. The sidecar reports capture/OCR latency, observed frame rate, CPU, and macOS physical footprint.
- Added a provider-neutral structured classification contract plus an ephemeral OpenRouter adapter for `openai/gpt-5.6-luna`, with reasoning disabled, strict JSON output, one request in flight, and newest-context replacement.
- Split local sensing from remote inference: local OCR/app/window metadata goes first after a one-second settle period; changed text context is throttled to five seconds; unchanged dwell is reconsidered after 30 seconds; a low-detail JPEG is encoded only when the text response requests it, at most once per context fingerprint and no more than once per 30 seconds.
- Required OpenRouter parameter support, latency-priority routing, denied data-collecting providers, and required zero-data-retention routing. The sidecar records provider-returned token counts, per-call billed credits, and cumulative session credits; credit-purchase fees are separate.
- Compared the full local Codex harness with direct model endpoints. The controlled Codex/Luna image classification took 8.54 seconds and consumed 8,837 harness tokens, so it is retained only as evidence that a full agent harness is unsuitable for the sensing loop.
- Used the build and test commands recorded in `Tab/dist/reproduction.txt`.

### Observations

- All 37 deterministic tests pass on macOS 26.5.2 arm64. They cover the frozen eyes, launch opt-in, local change policy, capture lifecycle/display selection, remote settle/heartbeat policy, bounded metadata/OCR, strict request construction, privacy routing, usage-credit parsing, cancellation, and mocked transport failures.
- The user enabled Screen Recording for the stable signed `app.usefence.tab` identity. The exact packaged sidecar started ScreenCaptureKit without another prompt and produced its first local app/window/OCR row in 315 ms. A stop/start produced another current frame in 153 ms.
- A 60-s static-capture benchmark of the exact packaged process (`60` valid one-second samples) measured 1.255% mean CPU, 1.8% peak CPU, 27.058 MiB mean RSS, and 27.859 MiB peak RSS. Apple's `footprint` tool reported 30 MiB current physical footprint and 43 MiB peak. The app meter was changed to report `phys_footprint` rather than Mach `resident_size`, which had over-counted shared mappings.
- The existing packaged build reported `0.00 fps` between initial frames. Since the coordinator increments its counter before visual filtering, this means no processable frame reached it during those intervals; dynamic-frame delivery must be rechecked on the rebuilt capture source rather than interpreting the zero as duplicate suppression.
- After the capture lifecycle and request-race fixes, the exact packaged app rebuilt with the stable `app.usefence.tab` development identity, passed deep signature verification, and produced its first local row in 132 ms. Continuous changing-screen delivery remains a separate manual validation gate.
- No user API key was entered or transmitted. The live Luna latency and exact per-frame cost test remains pending a user-entered OpenRouter key.
- At the current direct Luna list price of $0.20 per million input tokens and $1.20 per million output tokens, a literal request every second for an eight-hour day means 28,800 calls. A representative metadata-only request of 250 input and 40 output tokens is about $2.82 per day or $62.09 for 22 working days. Adding a 256-token low-detail image to every request raises that estimate to about $4.30 per day or $94.53 per 22-day month, before OpenRouter's credit-purchase fee. Actual billing must be extrapolated from `usage.cost`, because provider routing, discounts, image tokenization, and output length can change.
- Apple exposes structured app/window identity and bounds through NSWorkspace, CoreGraphics window dictionaries, and ScreenCaptureKit shareable content, but not a general semantic JSON representation of the screen. Local Vision OCR supplies text. Browser URL/DOM data would require a browser extension or browser-specific Automation/Accessibility permission.
- The already-installed Peekaboo 3.2.3 CLI confirmed the value and cost of a combined Accessibility/capture snapshot: targeted Arc window metadata returned in 0.27 s, while full `see --json` capture plus AX traversal took 2.07–3.29 s and reached a 1,000-element/depth truncation boundary. This is useful as an AX-context benchmark, not as a one-hertz production subprocess.

### Interpretation

A one-call-per-second design is unnecessary and financially wasteful even with a nano-tier model. The appropriate architecture is event-driven: use frontmost-app/window changes, local OCR, and perceptual change locally; apply deterministic hard rules and a short dwell delay; ask a model only for ambiguous changed contexts; and send pixels only as a second-stage escalation. This also avoids a remote queue that narrates obsolete screens.

The full Codex harness is too slow and token-heavy for the hot loop. The first benchmark should compare stateless Luna, a warm OpenAI Realtime mini session, a warm Gemini Live session, and Groq Qwen vision using identical frames and time-to-valid-classification rather than raw output-token throughput.

### Next steps

- Validate continuing frames while switching and scrolling between non-Tab apps on the rebuilt signed artifact.
- Measure a changing-screen capture/OCR window after continuing delivery is verified. The completed 60-second static window is a lower bound. Exact Apple Energy Impact is unavailable non-root; use `powermetrics --show-process-energy` only if the user authorizes a privileged measurement.
- Have the user manually enter an OpenRouter key in the secure field, then record actual Luna latency, token counts, and `usage.cost` across representative app switches.
- Compare a bounded native AX snapshot with local OCR on representative Cocoa, browser, Electron, PDF, canvas, and video contexts. Keep Peekaboo as a benchmark/reference; preserve Tab's own permission identity in the product path.
- Build a small canonical frame set before choosing a production provider; measure classification quality and false-positive interventions alongside p50/p95 latency and cost.
