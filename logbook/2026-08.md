# August 2026

## 2026-08-01 — PER-414 Tab notch-companion prototype

### Objective

Test whether a permission-isolated companion can occupy the smallest practical area beneath a notched Mac display, follow the pointer, and express escalating concern without changing Fence's binary or permissions.

### Method

- Started from verified Fence 3.4.11 commit `8ce8e4f` in a separate clean checkout; the dirty `/Users/vishaljain/selfcontrol` reference checkout was preserved.
- Added the independent Xcode app `Tab.app` in implementation commit `d43b0a20c8c73766a860a69359d4e1fafac43830`.
- Derived placement from `NSScreen.safeAreaInsets` and the auxiliary top areas, then drew a 44 x 20-point AppKit panel.
- Polled `NSEvent.mouseLocation` at 30 Hz with exponential smoothing.
- Added click-cycled neutral, mild-concern, and very-concerned chibi expressions. Mild concern raises straight high-contrast inner grey brows; very concern rotates the full straight brows inner-down and adds smaller pupils, a brief wiggle, and four separate coral curves at the top-right temple with a state-only right gutter. Each curve is displaced 10 percent farther from the motif center and the motif is rotated 20 degrees clockwise.
- Built and tested using the commands in `Tab/dist/reproduction.txt`, then launched the exact packaged app and exercised the click cycle with Computer Use.

### Observations

- The built-in MacBook Pro display reported a 2056 x 1329-point screen, 38-point top safe area, and a 220 x 38-point physical-notch exclusion centered between the auxiliary areas.
- The live Tab window reported a compact 44 x 20-point neutral/mild footprint at status-bar window layer 25, directly beneath the housing. Very concerned alone extends 6 points to the right for a 50 x 20-point footprint while leaving the eye positions fixed.
- The pointer-following eyes rendered and moved without a permission prompt.
- Computer Use reported the complete click sequence `neutral -> mild concern -> very concerned -> neutral`; the user approved the initial eyes and mild-concern direction and requested the inverted very-concern brows.
- At the real 44 x 20-point render size, the first tiny translucent stress mark clipped at the top-right boundary and read as a red blur. A thicker four-elbow revision became visible but formed the wrong flower/star silhouette and obscured the right eye and brow.
- The supplied chibi reference resolved the intended mark: four disconnected rounded curves bowing toward an empty center. The first reference-matched placement used an 8-point gutter and kept the mark fully separate from the face.
- Direct visual feedback favored a more attached temple cue. Moving the hatch 4 points upward and 3 points left gives it approximately 20 percent overlap at the moving eye/brow boundary without hiding either feature. That overlap permits a 6-point gutter; the live very-concerned capture measured 50 x 20 pixels.
- A later visual pass found that the four curves still merged perceptually. Moving each complete curve radially outward by 10 percent—without changing its size or 1.8-point stroke—increased the negative space. The requested 45-degree rotation was refined interactively to a subtler 20 degrees clockwise before validation.
- The user clarified that “remove the curvature in the eye balls” referred to the eyebrows. Replacing both brow Bezier paths with straight round-capped segments preserved the mild/very slope inversion while making the expression crisper; the round eye shapes were unchanged.
- All 14 tests passed on macOS 26.5.2 arm64. Xcode emitted only its deployment-version warning for the bundled XCTest framework while testing a macOS 12 target.
- The packaged app's identifier is `app.usefence.tab`; its sole entitlement is `com.apple.security.app-sandbox`; deep signature verification passed with a local ad-hoc signature.

### Interpretation

The small notch-companion interaction is mechanically viable without Screen Recording, Accessibility, microphone, Automation, network, daemon, or Fence integration. Grey brows keep concern character-led, while the reference-matched coral hatch supplies a high-heat cue without turning the whole face into an alarm. At this scale, a controlled edge overlap makes the mark feel attached to the character and uses less space than either full separation or drawing over the face. Click-to-cycle is useful for UI tuning but should be revisited when a real accountability state machine owns expressions.

### Next steps

- Keep the user-approved three-tier UI frozen while backend context sensing is developed on a separate ticket and branch.
- Replace temporary click control with explicit state input once the heat model exists.
- Evaluate structured foreground-app/window context before sending pixels; gate ScreenCaptureKit and network access separately and do not add those permissions to Fence.
